Applied readings

Readings

The atlas indexes a field; this section uses one of its frameworks. The five essays below apply François Jullien's apparatus — écart, the détour, the commun, silent transformation, the universal/uniform/common distinction — to specific axes of the U.S.–China relation. They are presented here as one school's reading, not as the project's spine. Other school readings (Hall–Ames pragmatist, Mou New-Confucian, Slingerland cognitive, Zhao tianxia-IR) would yield other essays this set deliberately does not represent.

Each essay's footer notes the Jullien works it draws on; full bibliographic information on the Sources page.

Jullien-applied readings on US–China (5 essays)

  1. 1. Strategy and efficacy: 勢 shi against the plan

    Why U.S. national-security planning and PLA strategic writing produce different objects, and why the Western frame keeps under-reading the Chinese one.

  2. 2. Governance as silent transformation: 化 hua against the event

    What an event-organized media culture systematically misses about cumulative reorientation, and why China's rise was so often described as imminent rather than as ongoing.

  3. 3. Ethics as relational virtuosity: 仁 ren against the moral law

    Why human-rights debates run aground at a register the Western frame cannot recognize as ethics, and what an honest account of that écart would look like.

  4. 4. The grammars of time: 時 shi-seasonality against chronos and kairos

    Quarterly capitalism, electoral cycles, five-year plans, and dynastic horizons: how institutional time-keeping shapes what counts as strategic patience or strategic drift.

  5. 5. Universal, uniform, common: cultivating le commun across the écart

    Why the human-rights universalism of the post-1945 order over-claimed, why Chinese counter-claims of civilizational particularism over-defend, and what a third path might require.

Other readings the atlas would support

These essays could be extended by readings from other schools the atlas indexes. They are listed here as openings rather than written entries; the underlying framework for each is available on the relevant thinker and dispute pages.

  • Hall–Ames role-ethics readings on US–China governance

    If persons are constituted by relations and ethics is ars contextualis, what does that imply for governance, civil society, and U.S.–China dialogue? Not yet drafted.

  • Mou Zongsan and the rights debate

    The New Confucian engagement with Kant, applied to the question of whether human rights as currently institutionalized can survive a serious encounter with non-Western moral epistemology. Not yet drafted.

  • Slingerland on wuwei and U.S. management culture

    If wuwei is read in cognitive-control terms, the Chinese cultural elaboration of skill-acquisition and effortless performance is a global resource — including for management and engineering cultures with no claim on the philosophy. Not yet drafted.

  • Zhao Tingyang on tianxia and the international order

    What a serious philosophical engagement with the tianxia proposal would have to concede on both sides, and where the proposal can and cannot be made to do honest work. Not yet drafted.

  • Watsuji's aidagara and the diplomatic relation

    If ethics is fundamentally aidagara — the betweenness from which selves emerge — what does that imply for U.S.–China diplomatic culture, and how does it differ from the Jullien l'entre that descends from the same Chinese root? Not yet drafted.
