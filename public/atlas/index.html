An atlas of comparative philosophy across the Asia–West écart. Concepts (circles) come from the traditions; thinkers (rounded rectangles) work them — sometimes coining, sometimes critiquing, sometimes carrying them across the gap. Click any node to read a summary.

Eight disputes structure the field: the Needham question, the Asian Values debate, Mou Zongsan against Kant on intellectual intuition, Billeter against Jullien on method, role ethics versus rights, tianxia and the international order, the wuwei dispute, and the immanence/transcendence question itself. Each dispute is a structured position-map.

The starting hypothesis: U.S.–China becomes legible through neither convergence (we'll all become liberal) nor clash (we are simply opposed) but through the productive gap between configurations of thought that each become legible by the other.
